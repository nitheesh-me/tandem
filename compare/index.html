Comparison

Tandem compared with Teleparty, Syncplay, Discord, Plex and Jellyfin

Tandem Watch Party is one of several ways to watch videos together with friends far away. This page compares it with five others, and it tries to be fair: each of them does something Tandem does not.

The facts about the others come from their own pages, read on 9 November 2026, and the sources are listed at the end.

The short answer

Which one to pick

  • You have the film as a file and your friends are far away, each with their own screen: Tandem or Syncplay. Tandem needs no server and only one person needs the file. Syncplay needs everyone to have a copy and a player, but it works with the players people already use.
  • You want Netflix, Disney+ or another streaming service: Teleparty. Tandem cannot play streaming services at all.
  • You already run a media server: Jellyfin SyncPlay on your own server, or Plex Watch Together, which is now only in Plex's web app.
  • You want to talk while you watch, or show anything on your screen: Discord Go Live. Tandem has text chat and reactions, but no voice or video.

Fair to each

Where the others are better

Teleparty is the only one here for streaming services, which is what most people watch. It needs only an extension or its app, takes rooms of "up to 1000+" [1], and has voice and video with Premium. Tandem cannot do any of that, because it plays files you have.

Discord Go Live needs no copy of anything and nothing extra for people who already use Discord. Anything on the streamer's screen goes out, with voice and video in the same place [3]. Free streams are 720p at 30 fps [3]. Every viewer sees one compressed stream, so the picture is as sharp as the streamer's upload allows, and nobody can pick their own subtitles or language.

Plex and Jellyfin convert on a server, so any device plays anything in the library and nobody needs the file. For a home that already runs one, that is the simplest path. Plex said in February 2025 that Watch Together would stay only in its web app [6]. Jellyfin SyncPlay needs each viewer to have a user on the server [10], and its Android TV app does not have it yet [11].

Syncplay has been around since 2012, works inside players people already like, such as mpv, VLC and MPC-HC, and lets each person keep their own subtitles and audio [12] [13]. It does not move files, so everyone must already have the same film [13]. Its default thresholds keep the screens within a second or two [14], which is fine for a film night.

What it does

Where Tandem is different

  • No account and no server to run, and only one person needs the file. The others stream it from the room, and every piece is checked.
  • The same frame on every screen. Drift is trimmed with playback speed, and in tests on one computer the screens stayed about 10 ms apart.
  • The room carries on when its host leaves. People who join late start at the right moment, and the room waits up to 20 seconds for someone who is buffering.
  • Each person chooses their own audio language, subtitles and how they look, and picture size.
  • Every copy is compared, so a wrong file shows before you press play.
  • Almost any file plays, converted with FFmpeg on the device's graphics chip when needed.

Tandem is weaker in other ways: it plays no streaming services, has no voice or video, has no iPhone or Mac release yet, suits small rooms for full members, and converting a film is heavy on a computer without a working graphics chip. Its translations other than English are machine drafts.
